Coding the Comparator field

Three new options handle designs the old list couldn't express — dose-ranging studies and co-administration factorials. Here is when to tick each, worked through three real trials. For the whole template, see the full extraction guide.

What's new

Three options added

other dose levels of the intervention (dose-ranging)

Three or more dose levels compared, with dose as the variable under study.

intervention component alone (co-administration studies)

An arm giving the psychedelic without the co-administered drug.

co-administered component alone (co-administration studies)

An arm giving the co-administered drug without the psychedelic.

The rules

  • 1
    Tick everything that applies. This is a multi-select and most designs need two or three ticks. The options answer different questions, so they are not alternatives.
  • 2
    Low-dose vs dose-ranging are independent. Tick low-dose intervention drug when one arm is a designated low or near-inert dose serving as the control. Tick dose-ranging when three or more dose levels are compared. Tick both when both are true.
  • 3
    Placebo means genuinely inert. A low dose of the study drug is not a placebo, however small. If the lowest arm still contains the drug, don't tick placebo.

Worked examples

Three designs, coded

1 · Dose-ranging with a low-dose control

Goodwin 2022 · psilocybin for depression

Parallel-group, three arms: 25 mg, 10 mg and 1 mg psilocybin. The primary comparison was 25 mg vs 1 mg.

Arm What it is Tick
25 mg the therapeutic dose under test index — not a comparator
10 mg intermediate level; exists to characterise the dose–response dose-ranging
1 mg designated near-inert control dose, chosen to preserve the blind low-dose intervention drug dose-ranging
— there is no inert arm; every arm contains psilocybin not placebo

Tick: low-dose intervention drug · dose-ranging. Don't tick: placebo.

Both apply because they answer different questions — was there a designated control dose? (yes, 1 mg) and was dose systematically varied? (yes, three levels). A two-arm 1 mg vs 25 mg trial would be low-dose only.

2 · Factorial dose grid, two drugs

Äbelö 2025 · DMT + harmine PK/PD

Seven dose combinations of DMT and harmine (mg:mg) in a factorial design over six study days: 90:0, 90:60, 90:120, 90:180, 0:120, 60:120, 120:120.

Cell (DMT : harmine) What it is Tick
90:60 · 90:120 · 90:180
60:120 · 120:120		 the combination cells being characterised index — not comparators
90:0 DMT alone, without harmine intervention component alone
0:120 harmine alone, without DMT co-administered component alone
DMT 60/90/120
harmine 60/120/180		 both drugs are given at several levels — dose is the variable dose-ranging
(none) no cell has both drugs at zero not placebo

Tick: dose-ranging · intervention component alone · co-administered component alone. Don't tick: placebo, low-dose intervention drug.

No placebo cell. Every one of the seven cells contains at least one active drug — the "zero" in 90:0 and 0:120 is the other drug being absent, which is what makes those the component-alone arms. A factorial grid does not have to include an inert cell, and this one doesn't.

Note what is also not ticked: low-dose intervention drug. The low cells are rungs on the gradient, not a designated control.

3 · Co-administration 2 × 2

e.g. LSD + ketanserin

Four cells: LSD + ketanserin, LSD + placebo, placebo + ketanserin, placebo + placebo.

Cell What it is Tick
LSD + ketanserin the combination under study index — not a comparator
LSD + placebo intervention drug without the co-administered drug intervention component alone
placebo + ketanserin co-administered drug without the psychedelic co-administered component alone
placebo + placebo genuinely inert placebo

Tick: placebo · intervention component alone · co-administered component alone. Don't tick: dose-ranging — only one dose of each drug.

Some co-administration trials are only 2 cells. NCT04558294 in our database is just LSD + ketanserin-placebo vs LSD + ketanserin — so it gets intervention component alone only, with no placebo tick, because no inert cell exists.
